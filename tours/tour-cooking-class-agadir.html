Itinerary

This experience is built around simple, practical steps: visit the market, cook with the chef, and sit down to enjoy the meal together.

1

Market Visit & Ingredient Selection

30 minutes

Meet the chef, then make a short stop at a local market to see the spices and fresh ingredients used in Moroccan home cooking before heading into the class.

2

Hands-On Cooking - Prepare Your Moroccan Feast

2.5 hours

Back in the kitchen, the chef walks the group through the menu step by step. You help prepare the dishes, work with the spices, and stay involved throughout the cooking instead of just watching.

3

Enjoy Your Meal & Receive Recipes

1 hour

Once everything is ready, you sit down together and enjoy the meal you prepared. Before leaving, you get the recipes so the class stays useful after the day ends.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need cooking experience to join this class?

No. The class is beginner-friendly, and the chef guides the group step by step.

What dishes will we learn to cook?

The menu usually focuses on a few classic Moroccan dishes such as tagine, salads, and other home-style favorites. The exact dishes can vary by season and the day's ingredients.

Can dietary restrictions be accommodated?

Usually yes, as long as you mention allergies or dietary restrictions when booking.

Will I receive recipes to take home?

Yes. Recipe cards are included so you can recreate the dishes later.

A little more about the day

This is a relaxed, hands-on cooking class for travelers who want something more personal than a standard sightseeing tour. You join a local chef, make a short market stop, and learn how a few classic Moroccan dishes come together.

It works well for beginners because the class is guided step by step, and it stays practical rather than overly technical. By the end, you sit down to enjoy the meal and leave with recipes you can actually use again.

  • Arrive 10 minutes early for check-in
  • Total duration: 4-5 hours including market visit and meal

What to Bring

  • Clothing: Comfortable clothing suitable for cooking - you'll be standing, chopping, and moving around, so wear something comfortable. An apron is provided, but avoid loose sleeves or jewelry that could get in the way.
  • Photography: Camera or phone to capture your cooking experience - the market visit, cooking process, and finished dishes all make great photos. You'll want to remember this experience!
  • Note-taking: Small notebook for extra tips and notes (optional) - the chef shares lots of tips and variations that might not be on the recipe cards. Many guests find it helpful to take notes.
  • Appetite: Appetite for learning and enjoying Moroccan food! Come hungry - you'll be preparing a full meal and will enjoy eating it together. The portions are generous, so you'll definitely be satisfied.
  • Additional items: Cash for optional tips and any market purchases you might want to make, and an open mind ready to learn and have fun!
