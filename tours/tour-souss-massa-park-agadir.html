Frequently asked questions

What is the best time of year to visit Souss Massa National Park?

The best time to visit Souss Massa National Park for birdwatching is from October to April, when migratory birds from Europe and Africa are present. However, the park is beautiful year-round, and resident species like the bald ibis can be observed throughout the year. Spring (March-May) and autumn (September-November) offer the best combination of weather and bird diversity.

Do I need to be an experienced birder to enjoy this tour?

Not at all! This tour is designed for both experienced birdwatchers and nature enthusiasts of all levels. Our expert guides will help you identify birds, explain their behaviors, and share fascinating facts about the park's wildlife. Whether you're a beginner or an expert, you'll find the experience educational and enjoyable.

What wildlife can I expect to see besides birds?

While birds are the main attraction, Souss Massa National Park is also home to various mammals including wild boar, jackals, foxes, and occasionally gazelles. You may also spot reptiles like lizards and geckos, and the coastal areas sometimes reveal marine life. The park's diverse flora, including argan trees and various desert plants, adds to the natural beauty.

Is the tour suitable for children?

Yes, the tour is suitable for children aged 6 and above who can walk moderate distances and remain quiet during birdwatching. However, very young children (under 5) may find the tour challenging due to the need for patience and quiet observation. We recommend bringing snacks and activities to keep younger children engaged during quieter moments.

What should I wear for the tour?

Wear comfortable, lightweight clothing suitable for walking. Long sleeves and pants are recommended to protect from the sun and any vegetation. A wide-brimmed hat, sunglasses, and sturdy walking shoes or hiking boots are essential. Bring layers as temperatures can vary, especially near the coast. Neutral colors (beige, brown, green) are best for wildlife observation.

A little more about the day

This is a quieter half-day wildlife trip from Agadir with hotel pickup, a licensed guide, and small groups (max 8). In roughly 4–5 hours you reach Souss Massa's protected lagoons and coastal viewpoints, with binoculars and local context included. The focus is on real birdwatching and landscapes—not a rushed photo stop—so expectations stay honest if migratory birds move with the season.

Wildlife changes with the season, weather, and time of day, so we present this honestly as a guided birdwatching and wildlife experience, not a guaranteed checklist. In the right conditions you may spot flamingos, bald ibis, and other migratory birds, while your guide explains how the park changes across the year.

This tour suits travelers who would rather do one focused wildlife outing than another generic city or beach activity.   • Exact pickup time confirmed via WhatsApp the evening before
  • Please be ready 5 minutes before your scheduled pickup time
  • Return to hotel: Approximately 1:00-2:00 PM
  • Total tour duration: 4-5 hours including travel time

What to Bring

  • Footwear: Wear comfortable walking shoes for short outdoor stops on uneven ground.
  • Sun protection: Bring a hat, sunscreen, and sunglasses because much of the visit is outdoors.
  • Clothing: Light, breathable layers work best, especially in warmer months.
  • Water: Carry water with you during the trip.
  • Optional: Bring a camera or your own binoculars if you prefer.

Health & Safety

  • Stay on designated paths and follow guide instructions
  • Maintain a safe distance from wildlife (minimum 50 meters)
  • Do not feed or disturb animals or birds
  • Stay hydrated and protect yourself from sun exposure
  • Inform your guide of any medical conditions or allergies

Before You Book

  • Best birdwatching season: October to April (migratory birds)
  • Tour operates in all weather conditions; dress appropriately
  • Children under 5 years old may find the tour challenging
  • Photography is allowed but flash photography is prohibited near wildlife
  • This is a protected area; all visitors must respect conservation rules
